Market Stress ​

Volatility, credit and rates say the same thing more often than people notice. This read says it as one number, with a direction, every day.

The Question It Answers ​

"Is the pressure on this market building or coming off?"

The inputs are public and free. What nobody assembles for a reader is the direction of all of them at once. A feed covers VIX on the day VIX moves, credit spreads on the day a credit story breaks, and the yield curve when someone writes about a recession. Three separate days, three separate topics, no combined answer.

Why A Composite Beats Three Headlines ​

  • One number, every day, computed the same way. Comparable to yesterday and to last month, which is what makes a number worth following at all.
  • The direction is the message. Stress at 42 and falling is a different market from stress at 42 and rising. The post says which.
  • Stress leads the label. Rising stress is usually what precedes a regime downgrade, so it is the read that tells a reader to expect the bigger label to move.
  • Regime changes get their own alert. When the stress regime itself turns, that is published as a separate event, not buried inside the daily number.
  • The rule is public. The methodology names the inputs and the weights, so a reader can check whether they agree with the construction.

What The Post Looks Like ​

How It Is Built ​

A composite over volatility, high-yield credit, long rates and the yield curve, normalised so the number means the same thing across time rather than only against the last few sessions.
